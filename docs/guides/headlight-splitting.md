# Headlight Splitting

Getting a sealed headlight open without destroying it. This is the stage that
killed the project for a year, and it is still the stage most likely to end in
tears.

## Buy new headlights

The standard approach to splitting a sealed headlight is to heat it in an oven
until the sealing glue softens enough to prise the unit apart.

**You are strongly urged to buy a new set of headlights to work on.** New units
use a different glue compound, and/or the compound has not hardened to the point
where it can no longer be weakened. Several years on a car cures the OE glue
completely, and it becomes near impossible to melt.

Buying new also means your car stays driveable while you work, and you can sell
your old headlights on to recover some of the cost.

> Make sure you order the right headlights — LHD and RHD units differ, and
> different makes exist (Lucas and Depo are the most common).

See the [parts list](../parts-list.md) for suppliers and prices.

## Step 1 — Headlight prep

Strip the excess parts off the headlight first.

1. Remove the access caps from the rear of the headlight — the covers you twist
   off to reach the bulbs — and the small rubber tubes that act as ventilation
   and drains.
2. Flip the unit over and remove the **6 Torx screws** from the face plate. You
   need a **T-20** bit.
3. Lift off the black plastic cover, then lift off each of the three chrome
   covers to reveal the entire lens unit.

![Donor headlights](../images/build/donor-headlights-pair.jpg)

## Step 2 — Get cooking

No, really. This is the critical stage.

- Make sure whoever else lives in your house isn't around.
- Put the headlight on a baking tray.
- Set the oven to **155–175 °C**. Err on the side of caution — start low.
- Put the headlight in for **5–10 minutes**.
- Take it out and try to pull it apart, to see if there's any movement yet.
- Repeat until the glue is pliable.

> ⚠️ **If the oven is too hot, or the light is in too long, the perspex front
> lens will melt or distort** and the headlight is scrap. Take your time. Get it
> right. Low and slow beats one hot shot.

**Alternative:** use a hot air gun or blowtorch to focus heat directly on the
channel where the glue sits. In principle this is better — it keeps heat away
from the rest of the headlight — but it was never tried on 159/Brera/Spider
units during the project, so you'd be the test case.

## Step 3 — Prise and pry

Exactly as it sounds. Using a combination of tools — bojo bars, picks,
screwdrivers, glass removers — and good old-fashioned brute force, apply as much
pressure as you can while the headlight is still warm.

Work around the lip and the side clips. Once the seal breaks, the unit comes
apart easily.

If it cools before it gives, put it back in the oven rather than forcing it.

## The result

You end up with two main sections:

- The **black plastic rear** of the unit, carrying the bulb reflectors and the
  electronics.
- The **clear plastic lens section** with the internal chrome reflectors.

Plus the parts you took off during prep.

At this point you're ready to
[install the halo rings](ring-installation.md).
